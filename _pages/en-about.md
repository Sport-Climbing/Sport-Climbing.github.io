# About Sport Climbing

## A place to learn the sport and take it seriously

**Sport Climbing** exists to produce, gather and publish specialist writing on sport climbing. We want a place where people can find information they can trust, learn the subject properly and see it with a more scientific eye. The site is meant to do more than follow the news of the day. It should help the climbing community know the sport better.

Sport climbing is not only the moves on the wall or the result of a competition. Technique, exercise physiology, biomechanics, the psychology of performance, training principles, equipment, rules and research all belong in a real understanding of the sport.

## What we cover

The site follows the three competition disciplines as [World Climbing](https://www.worldclimbing.com/) defines them. The federation, formerly the IFSC, organises and develops competition climbing internationally.

The three disciplines are:

### Bouldering

Bouldering is climbing on short, technical problems. It asks for the ability to solve a sequence, and for strength, coordination, balance, precision and a good choice of moves.

### Lead

In lead, the climber moves up the route and clips the rope into the protection along the way. Endurance, the choice of moves, body position and decisions made while tired all shape the performance.

### Speed

Speed is about climbing a standard route in the shortest possible time. How fast the movement is executed, coordination, explosive power and the ability to repeat a performance are central.

These three disciplines are the core of what we publish. We also look at training, physical preparation, equipment, athletes’ health and the other scientific and technical sides of sport climbing.

For the official account of the disciplines, see [Inside World Climbing](https://www.worldclimbing.com/~/ifsc/inside-world-climbing).

## What we publish

### Technique and instruction

Our instructional writing covers movement, training principles, tactics, the skills each discipline asks for and ways of improving performance. It is written so that beginners, experienced climbers, coaches and anyone who wants to learn the subject in depth can use it.

### Research

Sport climbing is a field for research in exercise physiology, biomechanics, the psychology of performance, training adaptations, sports nutrition and injury prevention.

We try to turn relevant findings into writing that is clear, sourced and useful. That means citing serious research, paying attention to how a study was done and to its limits, and keeping scientific evidence separate from personal impression.

### Equipment and technology

We also look at equipment in technical terms: the tools used in training and competition, the standards that apply to them, and the technology that is changing the sport.

Here we try to separate technical information from advertising, and to judge equipment, as far as we can, on manufacturers’ documents, recognised standards and evidence that can be checked.

### Rules and competitions

The rules, the way events are run, scoring, judging and the structure of competitions are part of understanding sport climbing.

This part of the site explains official regulations, looks at the technical side of contests and examines how athletes perform, so that a result can be read as a sporting and technical event, not only as a ranking.

### News and events

Important news and events are covered too. Being first is not the priority. We would rather publish information that holds up, explain the setting of an event and add analysis once the result is known.

News therefore sits beside instruction, research and reference writing. It is one part of the site, not the whole of it.

## How we work

A specialist reference is only as good as the information in it, and only if that information can be checked. We hold to the following:

* **Reliable sources:** official documents, technical literature, specialist books and serious scientific research.
* **Citations:** readers should be able to follow a claim back to what it rests on.
* **Evidence kept apart from opinion:** research findings, personal experience, specialist advice and the author’s analysis are not the same thing, and should not be presented as if they were.
* **Scientific care:** method, the quality of the evidence and the limits of a study matter more than stretching a result past what it can support.
* **Responsibility:** particular care with anything that concerns training, health and safety.
* **Writing that lasts:** pieces people can learn from and come back to.

## Where we are headed

Sport Climbing aims to become a reliable reference for learning, research and a closer knowledge of sport climbing in English.

We want sound knowledge to be easier to reach, scattered sources to be set out in order, and experience on the wall, specialist teaching and scientific research to inform one another.

The sport does not develop only by adding ascents and competitions. It also needs continued learning, good teaching, accurate information and a critical look at what is already known.
